Autonomic Nervous System Learning Lab

Explore organ-specific sympathetic and parasympathetic pathways, vagal signaling, cardiovascular feedback, and respiration-linked heart-rate variation. These are conceptual simulations, not clinical instruments.

PATHWAY 01

Sympathetic outflow

Thoracolumbar preganglionic neurons synapse in sympathetic ganglia; postganglionic fibers reach many effectors. Adrenal medullary signaling is a related special case.

Transmitter patterns vary: acetylcholine at autonomic ganglia; norepinephrine at many postganglionic sympathetic targets, with important exceptions.

PATHWAY 02

Parasympathetic outflow

Cranial and sacral pathways project to ganglia near or within target organs. Acetylcholine is used at ganglia and most parasympathetic neuroeffector junctions.

The two divisions can act simultaneously and do not control every organ in simple opposition.

PATHWAY 03

Vagus nerve

The vagus contains extensive visceral sensory afferents as well as motor and autonomic efferents. Its cardiac efferent pathways can rapidly influence sinoatrial-node timing.

A wearable HRV score is not a direct measurement of vagal nerve firing.

PATHWAY 04

Baroreflex

Stretch-sensitive receptors in the carotid sinus and aortic arch signal pressure changes through cranial nerves IX and X to brainstem networks, which adjust autonomic cardiovascular output.

Feedback is dynamic and adapts; this simplified model does not estimate blood pressure.

SIMULATION 03 · HRV METRICS

Explore variability mathematically

Choose an illustrative sequence of normal-to-normal (NN) intervals and inspect mean heart rate, SDNN, and RMSSD. These are synthetic data, not Polar H10 readings.

SDNN describes overall interval dispersion; RMSSD emphasizes successive differences. Neither metric independently diagnoses autonomic health. Synthetic sequences have no ectopic beats or artifact corrections.
